# Periodization and deloads

Training hard indefinitely with no planned dip in stress doesn't work — fatigue is cumulative
and eventually outpaces adaptation even with perfect volume and RPE targets. Periodization is
just the general practice of deliberately varying training stress over weeks, and a deload is
its most important building block: a short, planned drop in volume and/or intensity that lets
fatigue dissipate before it forces an unplanned one (a plateau, a nagging injury, or burnout).

## When a deload is actually warranted

Not on a rigid calendar — triggered by signals:
- RPE creeping up for the same planned weight/reps across 2-3 consecutive sessions (the
  fatigue-vs-adaptation gap is widening).
- Reps missed on lifts that were previously solid.
- Several consecutive weeks run at or near MRV (see `volume-landmarks.md`) for a given muscle.
- Reported soreness/fatigue not resolving between sessions, or a logged rest day pattern that
  suggests the trainee is dragging.
- As a simple floor: most trainees training hard need a deload roughly every 4-8 weeks even
  without an obvious trigger, because fatigue accumulates even when it isn't consciously felt.

## What a deload actually looks like

Cut volume, not necessarily intensity — this is the version with the best evidence for
preserving strength/muscle while still letting fatigue clear:
- Roughly half the working sets per muscle group for that week.
- Keep load/intensity close to normal (RPE 5-6) rather than gutting the weight too, so skill
  and neural drive aren't lost.
- One week is typically enough; longer risks losing the training effect for no extra recovery
  benefit.

## How this should shape a generated program

- If the trainee's recent history shows rising RPE at the same weight, or missed reps
  reappearing across a few sessions, bias the next session toward a lighter, lower-volume day
  rather than pushing another overload attempt.
- Don't treat "the user trained hard 6 days this week" as automatically good — flag it as a
  candidate for a lighter day or a deload cue, not just more volume tomorrow.
- A single hard day after a rest day is normal and doesn't need a deload response; this is
  about a sustained pattern across a couple weeks, not one tough session.
