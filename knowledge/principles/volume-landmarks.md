# Volume landmarks (MEV / MAV / MRV)

Weekly working sets per muscle group aren't a single "right number" — they sit on a curve.
This is the shared framework behind most modern evidence-based hypertrophy coaching (the
MEV/MAV/MRV terms specifically come from research popularized by exercise scientist Dr. Mike
Israetel, but the underlying dose-response relationship is broader exercise-science consensus,
not one person's proprietary system).

- **MEV (Minimum Effective Volume)** — the least weekly volume that still produces a
  meaningful adaptation. Below this, you're maintaining, not growing.
- **MAV (Maximum Adaptive Volume)** — the sweet spot range where most weekly gains happen for
  most trainees most of the time. This is where a normal training week should usually sit.
- **MRV (Maximum Recoverable Volume)** — the ceiling. Past this, fatigue outpaces recovery and
  performance degrades even though "more sets" feels like it should mean more progress.

## Rough weekly set ranges per muscle group (intermediate trainee, direct sets, hard sets only)

| Muscle group      | MEV | MAV range | MRV  |
|--------------------|-----|-----------|------|
| Chest              | 8   | 12-20     | 22   |
| Back (lats/rows)   | 10  | 14-22     | 25   |
| Shoulders          | 8   | 12-20     | 24   |
| Quads              | 8   | 12-18     | 20   |
| Hamstrings/glutes  | 6   | 10-16     | 18   |
| Biceps             | 6   | 10-18     | 22   |
| Triceps            | 6   | 10-16     | 20   |
| Calves             | 8   | 12-18     | 22   |
| Abs/core           | 0   | 8-16      | 20   |

These are starting-point ranges, not fixed truths — beginners need less to grow, highly trained
lifters can often tolerate more, and life stress/sleep/nutrition shrink the ceiling on a given week.

## How this should shape a generated program

- A new trainee or someone training 2-3 days/week should sit near MEV-to-low-MAV per muscle,
  not chase MRV — they don't have the recovery capacity or technique yet to use it well.
- Someone training hard 4-6 days/week with a real history can run mid-to-high MAV.
- Nobody should be programmed at MRV every week indefinitely — that's a short-term push, not a
  steady state. Sustained MRV without a deload is the most common cause of stalled progress and
  nagging joint pain in otherwise well-designed programs.
- Count sets across the WEEK, not just the session — a muscle hit directly on Monday and
  indirectly (as a secondary mover) on Wednesday still accumulates fatigue on both days.
